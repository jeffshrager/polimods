### `homophily_polarization` experiment — the new per-experiment template

Potential Experiment 6 from the README: crosses `electorate_shape`
(`single-peaked` vs `two-camp`) into the homophily × social-influence sweep
that `network_sweep` only ever ran on a two-camp electorate. The question:
does homophily *manufacture* polarization from a neutral starting
distribution, or only *sharpen* a split that's already there?

500 runs (50 conditions × 10 repetitions), `run_metrics_every_step = true` so
`generic_analyses/` has dynamics to draw. Spec, manifest, and `runs.csv` are
committed; `steps.csv` and PNGs are gitignored per the existing convention
(regenerable from the spec).

**Finding:** homophily mostly doesn't manufacture. At the one influence level
where the signal is resolvable above noise (`social_influence = 0.05`),
`coalition_gap` (the separation between each party's actual supporters, not
just overall spread) rises with homophily 3–4× more in `two-camp` than in
`single-peaked` (pooled slope ratio ~10× on `coalition_gap`, ~4× on
`ideology_sd`). The negative control (`social_influence = 0`, homophily alone)
held flat as it should. Above `social_influence = 0.1`, `coalition_gap` has
already collapsed near zero in both shapes and any residual homophily effect
is within run-to-run noise — the finding rests on one influence level, and the
paper says so rather than overreaching from the noisier rows.

**Structure established, meant to generalize:**

```
experiments/<stamp>_<name>/
  <name>.toml           spec
  manifest.json, runs.csv   (steps.csv, *.png gitignored)
  analyses/              question-specific code + its own outputs (CSV, PNG, findings.md)
  paper/                 a written draft synthesizing the finding, with reproducibility info
```

`analyses/manufacture_vs_sharpen.py` reads `runs.csv` + `steps.csv` directly
(no pandas — the project's existing style throughout `generic_analyses/` is
csv + numpy), computes the coalition-gap metric this specific question needed
(not a generic one), and writes its own CSV/PNG/markdown outputs into
`analyses/`. `paper/paper.md` is a ~1500-word draft built only from numbers
actually in `analyses/condition_summary.csv` — abstract, motivation, design,
metrics, results with a real numbers table, discussion, an honest limitations
section (flagging that the core claim rests on one influence level), and a
reproducibility block with the git commit and exact rerun commands.

Commit `69854c0`.

### Paper framing correction — worth remembering for future papers

The first paper draft reported the right numbers but buried the finding. It
called the `social_influence = 0` flat line (`coalition_gap` 0.36–0.73,
unmoved by `homophily`) "the negative control holding" — true, but that flat
line is where essentially all of the model's actual polarization lives. Every
other cell in the sweep collapses to 5–6% of that magnitude regardless of
`homophily`; the homophily-is-3–4×-stronger-in-two-camp result only describes
the shape of that already-collapsed residual, not a difference between
polarized and unpolarized. The user caught this directly ("homophily positive
and social influence 0 appears to be the only case where actual polarization
occurs") and asked why the paper hadn't led with it. Rewrote the abstract,
restructured §4 to state it first, and revised discussion/conclusion to
match — plus added the structural reason it's true (homophily only enters the
model through the term `social_influence` scales, so it's causally inert
when `social_influence = 0`, not just empirically flat). **Lesson:** when a
"negative control passes as expected" cell turns out to have the largest
effect size in the whole sweep, that's the headline, not a footnote — check
which cell actually has the largest magnitude before writing the narrative
around the cells that vary.

Then embedded the two `analyses/` figures (`coalition_gap.png`,
`ideology_sd.png`) directly into the paper at the point each is argued for,
since the coalition_gap plot is the visual proof of the corrected finding —
one flat high line against four flat low ones. This required a narrow
`.gitignore` exception (`!experiments/**/paper/*.png`) alongside the existing
blanket `experiments/**/*.png` rule: figures a `paper/` embeds are the
delivered document, not regenerable scan output, so they get committed as
copies inside `paper/` specifically, while `analyses/` and the top-level
per-condition figures stay gitignored as before.

Commits `fba06d3`, `4da4d29`.

### Interpretive discussion, not yet acted on

User proposed reading the finding as "echo chambers (low social pull) vs. the
diverse real world (high social pull)." Pushed back: that maps "diversity of
contacts" onto `social_influence`, but diversity-of-contacts is actually what
`homophily` encodes — the parameter this experiment found to be the *weak*
one. The model's more literal claim is closer to the reverse of the intuitive
story: any social-updating channel at all, whether toward similar or
different neighbors, drags the population toward consensus; `homophily` only
changes the outcome by single-digit percent. What preserves separation is
having no updating channel operating at all, not being surrounded by
agreement.

Asked whether this correlates with a real phenomenon: the honest answer is
that it reproduces a known *class* of behavior in the opinion-dynamics
literature — DeGroot/Friedkin-Johnsen-style averaging models generically
converge to consensus under any positive influence weight on a connected
network, regardless of topology; persistent disagreement in that literature
normally requires "stubborn"/zealot agents who never update, or a
bounded-confidence rule (Hegselmann–Krause) where agents only average with
neighbors close enough in opinion. This model has neither — every voter
averages toward *all* neighbors regardless of ideological distance, and
`homophily` only shapes who the neighbors are, not how influence is
discounted by disagreement. That is the structural reason homophily turned
out weak here. Flagged Bail et al. (PNAS 2018) — cross-partisan social media
exposure sometimes *increasing* polarization — as a real-world pattern this
model cannot reproduce, since its update rule only ever pulls voters toward
neighbors, never away.

**Possible next experiment, proposed but not built:** add a
Hegselmann–Krause-style bounded-confidence term (ignore neighbors beyond some
ideological distance rather than averaging in everyone) as a way to let
`homophily` actually matter on its own terms instead of being swamped by
uniform averaging. Nothing implemented — this is a live idea for whenever the
general model or a new sweep picks it up.
